\begin{abstract}
This manuscript specifies an exploratory, reproducible protocol for evaluating probability calibration of a deterministic CPU nearest-centroid activity-recognition baseline on the Human Activity Recognition Trondheim (HARTH) dataset. It is not a report of completed experiments. The primary design is nested subject-held-out evaluation, with inner-subject temperature selection, subject-cluster bootstrap uncertainty, and preregistered back-only and thigh-only sensor ablations. Every prospective result is gated on frozen provenance, leakage checks, class coverage, finite metrics, and a validated machine-readable artifact. Until those gates pass, tables and figures render explicit \status{UNVERIFIED} placeholders and no strong claim is permitted. The bounded related-work corpus contains metadata-only evidence and is likewise not sufficient to establish novelty or transfer claims.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Activity-recognition probabilities are useful only when their uncertainty is interpreted in the population and measurement setting in which they are produced. HARTH is a human-activity dataset recorded with two accelerometer placements; the landed metadata record describes the dataset and its subject-held-out baseline context \cite{harth}. The present episode asks a narrower question: can a frozen, deterministic CPU baseline produce useful held-out-subject probabilities after calibration? This manuscript deliberately separates that question from deployment, clinical, causal, and universal-calibration claims.

The title and status banner are intentionally conservative. The current episode is \status{INCONCLUSIVE}; real-data execution is opt-in, raw data are not committed, and protocol adjudication reported no real-data rerun. Thus this paper documents a protocol and manuscript mechanics, not an empirical conclusion.

\section{Protocol-v2 Methods}
\subsection{Estimand and split}
The primary estimand is expected per-window test loss for a new subject drawn from the finite eligible HARTH subject set. Losses are first averaged within subject and then macro-averaged across outer test subjects. A pooled-window summary is descriptive only. The primary split is leave-one-eligible-subject-out; all sessions for a subject remain in that fold, and no window crosses a subject or session boundary.

Eligibility, the subject list, class vocabulary, file manifest, preprocessing version, and protocol hash must be frozen before a result file is opened. Any changed input, ambiguous identifier, invalid provenance, or post-hoc split revision is a hard failure.

\subsection{Model and calibration}
The sole primary model is the existing deterministic CPU nearest-centroid baseline. The frozen representation uses six accelerometer channels (back and thigh), with per-channel window mean and standard deviation. Back-only and thigh-only features are exactly two pre-registered secondary ablations. Scaling, centroids, and every statistic are fit on outer-training subjects only.

For each outer fold, inner leave-one-subject-out validation on outer-training subjects supplies logits for a bounded scalar temperature fit. The optimizer minimizes inner-validation NLL over $T\in[0.05,20]$, with fixed tolerance and ties resolved toward smaller $T$. The selected temperature is then applied once to the refit outer-training model and outer test subject. If class coverage or inner validation fails, calibration is unavailable for that fold and is recorded as a failure rather than repaired with test data.

\subsection{Metrics and uncertainty}
NLL, multiclass Brier score, and top-label ECE with ten equal-width bins on $[0,1]$ are primary. Accuracy, macro-F1, recall, and precision are secondary context. Uncertainty uses 2,000 subject-cluster bootstrap resamples with seed 0 and percentile 95\% intervals. Paired calibrated--uncalibrated differences are reported per subject. The three primary comparisons use preregistered Holm correction at family-wise $\alpha=0.05$; ablation p-values are exploratory.

\subsection{Stopping and failure rules}
The run budget is one nested-LOSO primary run, three sensor configurations, two calibration states, and the required inner fits, capped at 22 eligible outer folds and 30 minutes on the declared CPU environment. Leakage, crossed windows, missing provenance, invalid probabilities, non-finite metrics, failed class coverage, changed frozen input, or timeout stops the run. Failed and inconclusive records remain immutable under \texttt{artifacts/}.

\section{Limitations}
This is not a systematic review, a deployment evaluation, or evidence of general calibration. The bounded metadata corpus is incomplete and its evidence spans are UNVERIFIED. Subject count, missing classes, sensor placement, finite-sample uncertainty, ECE sparsity, baseline choice, and the protocol's CPU assumptions constrain interpretation. The legacy S006-to-S015--S022 arrangement is a same-data exploratory diagnostic and cannot become confirmatory through relabeling.
