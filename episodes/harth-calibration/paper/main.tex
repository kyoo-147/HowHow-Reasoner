\begin{abstract}
We report a custody-released, exploratory analysis of a deterministic CPU nearest-centroid activity-recognition baseline on HARTH~\cite{harth}. The frozen population and all run quantities are generated from the exact result and displayed through macros: \resultSubjects{} subjects, \resultFolds{} lifecycle folds, \bootstrapReplicates{} bootstrap replicates, and \signFlipDraws{} sign-flip draws. Nested subject-held-out evaluation compares uncalibrated probabilities with inner-subject temperature scaling for full, back-only, and thigh-only sensor representations. Full-sensor calibrated NLL is generated in Table~\ref{tab:metrics}; Holm adjustment yields no familywise rejection. Exploratory support-aware F1 preserves \texttt{NOT\_ESTIMABLE}. This inconclusive report makes no novelty, causal, superiority, transferability, or generalization claim.

\section{Introduction}
Probability quality matters when activity-recognition systems communicate uncertainty, but a held-out-subject estimate is not a deployment guarantee. This report answers a narrow question: how did a frozen deterministic CPU baseline behave under the released HARTH run3 analysis, with and without inner-subject temperature scaling? The analysis is bounded to a frozen population and declared estimands. It does not estimate clinical utility, real-time safety, or population-wide calibration.

\section{Related Work}
HARTH is a public free-living activity-recognition dataset with subject-held-out baseline evaluation and two accelerometer placements~\cite{harth}. Work on confidence-calibrated wearable activity recognition motivates treating probability quality separately from class accuracy~\cite{harcal}. Calibration studies also use proper scoring rules, including NLL and Brier score, while emphasizing that calibration behavior can vary with the evaluation distribution~\cite{proper}. These three references are a bounded, non-systematic literature slice: their metadata and landing-page evidence do not establish transfer to HARTH, novelty, priority, or a literature-wide gap. Accordingly, this paper reports a candidate protocol and preserves the possibility that its observations are dataset- and split-specific.

\section{Methods}
\subsection{Design and estimands}
The primary estimand is the subject-macro expected test loss under leave-one-subject-out evaluation. All windows from a held-out subject remain in that subject's outer fold. The frozen population and three sensor configurations yield \resultFolds{} lifecycle folds. The baseline is deterministic nearest-centroid classification over channel means and standard deviations. Calibration selects a bounded scalar temperature only with inner training-subject validation, then applies it once to the outer test subject.

\subsection{Metrics, inference, and support}
NLL, multiclass Brier score, and ten-bin top-label ECE are summarized per subject and then macro-averaged. The generator reads the strict result and emits every displayed numeric cell; no metric or run quantity is hand-entered in this manuscript. Intervals are percentile subject-cluster bootstrap intervals using \bootstrapReplicates{} replicates. The three calibration comparisons use Holm adjustment at the declared familywise threshold. Sign-flip tests use the retained draw count of \signFlipDraws{} with the protocol's tie and zero rules. Sensor ablations are descriptive. F1 is computed only where TP/FP/FN support permits estimation; absent support is retained as \texttt{NOT\_ESTIMABLE}.

\subsection{Version 2.1 support and inference}
The v2.1 support policy binds each lifecycle fold to a frozen subject and configuration/state pairing, separates calibrated from uncalibrated arms, and records support-aware classwise outcomes. Primary inference is limited to the three paired calibration hypotheses (NLL, Brier, ECE). The generated table reports raw p-values, Holm-adjusted p-values, and decisions; it does not treat a confidence interval crossing zero as a positive or negative proof. Ablation contrasts are outside that primary family and remain descriptive. The prior terminal v2 attempt ended in a support-policy failure and is not merged into these results; v2.1 is a protocol revision, not a claim that the earlier failure was scientifically resolved.

\section{Discussion and Custody Dissent}
The released evidence is balanced and inconclusive. Temperature scaling coincided with a negative full-sensor NLL contrast, but multiplicity adjustment removed familywise support, and Brier/ECE did not show a consistent benefit. Ablations show descriptive NLL differences for reduced representations, but the design cannot attribute them causally to a sensor. The custody dissent is retained: \texttt{final.json} reports COMPLETE while \texttt{scientific\_metrics=false} and \texttt{completed\_folds=[]}, whereas the completed engine snapshot records \resultFolds{} lifecycle folds. This discrepancy is disclosed rather than reconciled silently. These observations establish no novelty, superiority, transferability, or generalization.

\section{Limitations}
Only the frozen \resultSubjects{} subjects and \resultFolds{} folds are represented. Subject-level sufficient statistics and sign counts are not retained, so exact independent regeneration of every bootstrap interval and paired-test value is unavailable. The lifecycle metadata defect and unchanged quarantine flag are not scientific evidence. The bounded references are not a systematic review. Stronger evidence would require prospective hypotheses, corrected lifecycle metadata, governed retention of sufficient statistics and sign counts, and independent validation. None is performed here; run3 is not rerun or tuned.
